\begin{lemma}[Local geometry of repeated successors]\label{lem:local-reversal}
Fix a terminal forced pair $F=\{u,v\}$, and assume that each
predecessor source pair has Hamming distance at least $n-1$.
Then $d_{\rm H}(u,v)$ equals $n-1$ or $n-2$, and at most two
pairwise vertex-disjoint source pairs can force $F$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For terminal distance $n-1$, the updated words have one agreement
coordinate, which must be the overwritten position. Its two original
bits can be chosen independently. The four possible predecessor
pairs form the edges of a four-cycle on the four possible original
endpoints; a vertex-disjoint subfamily has size at most two.
For terminal distance $n-2$, the update must use one of the two
agreement coordinates, restoring a source distance of $n-1$ by
changing exactly one endpoint. There are two choices of coordinate
and two choices of endpoint. The four possible predecessor pairs
again form a four-cycle under endpoint incidence, with matching
number two. Thus no three disjoint pairs in an initial matching
can force the same successor.
\end{proof}
